**Introduction**

The German-West African Centre (G-WAC) for Global Health and Pandemic Prevention hosted at the Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology (KNUST), Kumasi, Ghana is offering this modular short course on Modelling for Pandemic Preparedness and Response (MPPR). The course is designed and implemented by G-WAC \- a cooperation between the KNUST, Berlin School of Public Health (with Technische Universität Berlin and Charité Universitätsmedizin Berlin) and University Hospital Bonn (and University of Bonn). G-WAC and the MPPR short course is supported by the German Academic Exchange Service (DAAD) with funding from the budget of the German Federal Foreign Office, Project ID: 57592595\.

**Course Description**

The emergence of the COVID-19 pandemic highlighted the lack of disease modelling capacity in many low-or middle-income countries (LMICs) including sub-Saharan Africa (SSA). In SSA, limitations of data and the generalised nature of globally produced models for pandemics weakens their utility and raises concerns over their accuracy. The availability of high-quality modelling evidence to inform decisions and the capacity of policymakers to use this evidence is therefore critically important. Additionally, modelling expertise is not only of value in epidemic/pandemic contexts but can play an important role in the management of endemic diseases. The course will introduce participants to the concept of modelling and their applicability in Ghana and other similar settings in Africa. Course content will be delivered through teaching and hands-on exercises to give the participants a comprehensive training on modelling.

As a modular course, the Modeling for Pandemic Preparedness and Response (MPPR) course is organized into two parts:

**Part I: Essentials of Disease Modelling**   
**Part II: Modelling for Pandemic Preparedness and Response**

**Course Objectives/Learning Outcomes**

At the end of the course, participants will be able to:

* Understand the concepts of infectious-disease transmission and control.  
* Enhance knowledge about data sources, data access, and data curation in (West) Africa.  
* Design, implement and use context-specific mathematical models, using the R programming language.  
* Improve knowledge of communication about modeling output.  
* Become part of a network of disease modelers with an interest in West Africa.

**Target Participant**

The course is targeted at interdisciplinary participants with strong quantitative background (at least a master’s degree or equivalent work experience) in mathematics, econometrics, epidemiology, data science, biomedical sciences, or other relevant disciplines.

**Certification**  
Upon successfully completing **BOTH course modules**, participants will be awarded a certificate issued by KNUST which is accredited for Continuous Professional Development (CPD) points and may be applicable to individuals who are members of or are associated with (health) professional bodies in Ghana or elsewhere. 

**Course Outline**

The course is a 9 days intensive in-person training hosted at the KNUST, Kumasi, Ghana. Part I of the course covers an estimated total workload of 24 hours, running over three (3) days, starting from **1st to 3rd September 2025**. Part II covers a workload of 48 hours, running over 6-days from **4 to 10 September 2025**, culminating in a pre−conference workshop at G-WAC’s final conference, organized in collaboration with the KNUST College of Health Sciences annual conference, and a launch event for a network of West African modellers.
